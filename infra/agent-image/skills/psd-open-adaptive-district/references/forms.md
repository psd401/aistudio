## Form A — Build plan: Open Adaptive District

- URL: https://docs.google.com/forms/d/e/1FAIpQLSfEpYbPG7SLF2GLFwe-Q5GuU5SrUOV7InZ3BhPdhKUD6Lv6pw/viewform
- Displayed title: Build plan: Open Adaptive District
- Access: requires a Google sign-in.

### Description (verbatim)

> Every team files one build plan at the start of each cycle, by the Friday of week 1. It records what your team will work on for the next six weeks and how you will know whether it worked.
>
> Before you start:
> • Post your build plan in your team’s Google Chat space first. This form asks the same questions, so you can copy your answers across.
> • Your PSD AI Agent can help you write it. Try asking: “Help me write a one-page build plan. Our goal is ___, our problem is ___, and we want to try ___.”
> • Keep it short. Two or three sentences per question is plenty, and you can change the plan as you learn.
>
> If your plan is already written, this takes about 10 minutes. The full template is in the Playbook: https://psd401.ai/open-adaptive-district/playbook

### Item 1: This cycle's focus, from Superintendent Krestin Bahr

- Type: Title/description (no input)

Help text:

> This cycle is about exploration and innovation: learn what the AI tools we already have can do, and use them to improve one process or practice that serves your team’s goals. The tools are changing faster than any training plan can keep up with, and the quickest way to learn them is to use them on real work. Whatever you build, student data privacy and the relationships our schools are built on come first.
>
> Choose a build that fits this focus.

### Item 2: Team name

- Type: Short answer
- Required: Yes

Help text:

> A short name other teams will recognize. Examples: “Transportation office”, “Middle school counselors”, “Executive cabinet”.

### Item 3: School or department

- Type: Short answer
- Required: Yes

Help text:

> Where your team works. If your team spans more than one school or department, list them all.

### Item 4: Cycle

- Type: Short answer
- Required: Yes

Help text:

> Which cycle this plan is for. This fall’s first cycle is Cycle 1, so enter 1.

### Item 5: Team lead

- Type: Short answer
- Required: Yes

Help text:

> Full name of the person who owns the build and keeps it moving week to week.

### Item 6: Scribe

- Type: Short answer
- Required: Yes

Help text:

> Full name of the person who posts your team’s check-in in your Chat space every Friday: what you tried, what you learned, where you’re stuck, and what you need.

### Item 7: Sponsor

- Type: Short answer
- Required: Yes

Help text:

> Full name of the leader who helps when your team gets stuck, usually whoever oversees your department. Let them know you’ve listed them.

### Item 8: Other team members

- Type: Paragraph
- Required: No

Help text:

> Everyone else on the team, one name per line. Teams have three to six people, counting the lead and the scribe. Your sponsor is not part of the count.

### Item 9: Google Chat space

- Type: Short answer
- Required: Yes

Help text:

> Paste the link to your team’s space. To copy it, open the space in Google Chat, click the space name at the top, and choose Copy link. If you can’t find it, type the space’s exact name.

### Item 10: Your build plan

- Type: Section break

Help text:

> These are the same questions as the build plan template in the Playbook. If your team has already posted its plan in its Chat space, copy the answers from there.

### Item 11: The goal it serves

- Type: Paragraph
- Required: Yes

Help text:

> Which goal you already have will this build help you reach? Name the goal itself; you’ll list tools later. It might be a school improvement goal, a department goal, an MTSS commitment, or a Peninsula 2030 priority.
>
> Example: “Our school improvement goal of getting more 9th graders on track in math.”

### Item 12: The problem

- Type: Paragraph
- Required: Yes

Help text:

> What is getting in the way right now, specifically, in your building or department? Say who it affects and roughly how much time or effort it costs. Two or three sentences.
>
> Example: “Our counselors spend about four hours a week rewriting the same schedule-change emails to families, and some families still reply confused.”

### Item 13: Our build

- Type: Paragraph
- Required: Yes

Help text:

> Finish this sentence: “We’re going to build ___, because we think it will ___.” A build can be a prompt your team reuses, an assistant or tool in AI Studio, a knowledge repository, or a new way of working with your PSD AI Agent. Keep it small enough to finish in one cycle.
>
> Example: “We’re going to build an AI Studio assistant that drafts schedule-change emails from our notes, because we think it will cut that time in half and make the emails clearer.”

### Item 14: Tools we'll try

- Type: Paragraph
- Required: Yes

Help text:

> Which AI tools will you use or test? For example your PSD AI Agent, AI Studio, a knowledge repository, or another district-approved tool. Naming them now lets your wrap-up say what each one was good for.

### Item 15: How we'll know

- Type: Paragraph
- Required: Yes

Help text:

> How will you know by week 6 whether it worked? Give two or three signs of success, and make at least one of them something you can count. Then name one thing that must not get worse.
>
> Example: “Schedule-change emails take under an hour a week. Fewer than two families a week reply confused. Families still feel they are hearing from a real person.”

### Item 16: Week-one plan

- Type: Paragraph
- Required: Yes

Help text:

> What will your team do this week? Plan only week 1; you’ll plan the rest as you learn.
>
> Example: “Collect ten recent emails, write the assistant’s instructions, and test it on three of them.”

### Item 17: Not this cycle

- Type: Paragraph
- Required: No

Help text:

> Optional. What are you deliberately leaving out, so the build stays small enough to finish?
>
> Example: “Staff emails and changes to the master schedule wait for a later cycle.”

### Item 18: What it could become

- Type: Paragraph
- Required: No

Help text:

> Optional. If this works, what could other teams in the district use? Usually a skill in the PSD AI Agent or a tool in AI Studio. One sentence is enough; you’ll know more by week 6.

### Item 19: Sign-off

- Type: Section break

Help text:

> One last check before you submit.

### Item 20: Kris Hagel's agent

- Type: Checkboxes
- Required: Yes
- Options:
  - Yes, agnt_hagelk@psd401.net is in our team’s Google Chat space.

Help text:

> Kris Hagel’s agent reads every team’s Chat space on Sunday afternoons. It tells teams when they are working on the same problem and flags any team that has gone quiet. It can only do that if it’s in your space. To add it, open your space in Google Chat, choose Add people & apps, and enter agnt_hagelk@psd401.net.
