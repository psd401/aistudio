# Build Plan

*Paper #1 of 3. Written in week 1, posted in the team's chat space by Friday. One page. Written fast — it's a starting point, not a contract.*

## The template — seven fields, verbatim from The Playbook

> **Team: ___ Cycle: ___ Date: ___**
>
> **The problem.** Two or three sentences. What's eating time, causing friction, or not working — specifically, in your building or department?
>
> **Our build.** "We're going to build ___, *because we think it will* ___."
>
> **How we'll know.** Two or three signs of success. At least one you can count. And name the thing that must NOT get worse ("…and families still feel personally communicated with").
>
> **Week-one plan.** Just the first week. You'll adjust the rest as you learn — that's the point.
>
> **Not this cycle.** What you're deliberately NOT taking on. Your polite way of keeping the build small enough to finish.
>
> **What it could become.** If this works, what do we hand everyone else? Usually: a skill added to the PSD AI Agent, or a tool in AI Studio. One sentence is plenty — you'll know more by week 6.

## Drafting notes

- **Keep it to one page.** If it won't fit, the build is too big — that's two builds. Split it.
- **"Our build" is one sentence, and it gets quoted verbatim in the Wrap-Up.** Write it so it still makes sense six weeks later.
- **At least one success sign must be countable.** "Teachers like it better" is not a sign; "drafting time drops from ~28 minutes to under 12" is.
- **The must-NOT-get-worse line is required, not optional.** A build that improves one thing by wrecking another has failed its own criteria.
- **Good problems live inside goals the team already has** — a smoother MTSS huddle, scaffolds built faster so the bar stays at grade level, family communication that actually lands.
- **It does not need to be pretty.** Substance over polish, every time.
