# Build plan

*Week 1. One page, written quickly; you will change it as you learn. Posted in the team's chat space by Friday of week 1, along with the first check-in. Peninsula teams then file it with the build plan form by that same Friday.*

## The template — verbatim from The Playbook (psd401.ai/open-adaptive-district/playbook)

> **Team:** \_\_\_ **Cycle:** \_\_\_ **Date:** \_\_\_
>
> **Lead:** \_\_\_ **Scribe:** \_\_\_ **Sponsor:** \_\_\_
>
> **The goal it serves.** Which goal you already have does this support? For example, a school or department improvement goal, an MTSS commitment, or a priority in your strategic plan.
>
> **The problem.** Two or three sentences. What is eating time, causing friction, or not working, specifically, in your building or department?
>
> **Our build.** “We’re going to build \_\_\_, because we think it will \_\_\_.”
>
> **Tools we’ll try.** Which AI tools will you use or test? Name them, so the wrap-up can say what each one was good for.
>
> **How we’ll know.** Two or three signs of success, at least one of which you can count. Also name one thing that must not get worse, for example “families still feel personally communicated with.”
>
> **Week-one plan.** Just the first week. You will plan the rest as you learn.
>
> **Not this cycle.** What you are deliberately leaving out, so the build stays small enough to finish.
>
> **What it could become.** If this works, what would everyone else get? Usually a skill added to the PSD AI Agent or a tool in AI Studio. One sentence is enough; you will know more by week 6.

## Worked examples — verbatim from the build plan form (illustrative, not a real PSD build)

- **The goal it serves:** “Our school improvement goal of getting more 9th graders on track in math.”
- **The problem:** “Our counselors spend about four hours a week rewriting the same schedule-change emails to families, and some families still reply confused.”
- **Our build:** “We’re going to build an AI Studio assistant that drafts schedule-change emails from our notes, because we think it will cut that time in half and make the emails clearer.”
- **How we’ll know:** “Schedule-change emails take under an hour a week. Fewer than two families a week reply confused. Families still feel they are hearing from a real person.”
- **Week-one plan:** “Collect ten recent emails, write the assistant’s instructions, and test it on three of them.”
- **Not this cycle:** “Staff emails and changes to the master schedule wait for a later cycle.”

A build can be a prompt the team reuses, an assistant or tool in AI Studio, a knowledge repository, or a new way of working with the PSD AI Agent (build plan form, "Our build").

## Drafting notes

- **One page.** If it won't fit, the idea is two builds — split it.
- **"The goal it serves" names the goal itself**, not the tools; tools go under "Tools we'll try".
- **"Our build" is one sentence** in the form above. It gets quoted in the wrap-up, so write it to still make sense in week 6.
- **At least one sign of success must be countable.** Push back on "teachers will like it better"; ask for the number.
- **Name one thing that must not get worse.** It is part of the template, not optional.
- **Good problems sit inside goals the team already has** — a smoother MTSS meeting, scaffolds built faster so every student gets grade-level work, family messages that people actually read.
- **Rough is fine.** It does not need to be polished.
